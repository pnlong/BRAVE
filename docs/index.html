BRAVE

Low-latency neural audio codecs for instrumental performance — and a path from body-tap input to out-of-domain timbres like water, fire, rain, and birdsong.

Approach

Start from a target-domain BRAVE model. Playing tap audio into it directly is the status quo — and usually fails. We compare three ways of adapting the same tap input toward a chosen timbre.

1 · Zero-shot

Train BRAVE only on the target domain (for example babbling brook). At inference, feed tap straight into that model: encode, then decode. No adapter. Out-of-domain taps often land off the target manifold — wrong timbre, ringing, or collapsed texture.

tap → EncY → DecY

2 · Manual EQ + reverb

Keep the same frozen target model, but preprocess the tap with hand-tuned EQ and a short reverb so its spectrum and room cue look closer to in-domain audio. Interpretable and fast to try — brittle across mics, players, and gain.

tap → EQ / reverb → EncY → DecY

3 · Separate CycleGAN

Train a tap encoder as well. Learn a latent warp from tap space into the target latent space, then decode with the target generator. No hand DSP at inference — the model learns the transfer.

tap → EncX → Wxy → DecY

Examples

Loudness sidechain

Loudness sidechain (default on, as in the realtime host) scales each reconstruction by a causal envelope of the tap, so quiet gaps stay quiet instead of filling with model hiss. Toggle it off to hear the raw model output.

Playing one clip pauses the others. Peak-normalized MP3s for fair A/B listening — not a claim about training loudness.
